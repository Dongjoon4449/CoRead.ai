Long-term AI Interactions Assignment
Compared to other AI chatbots, the most distinctive feature of Replika is its specialization in fostering companionship. While general-purpose AI systems such as ChatGPT or Gemini serves a wide range of functions - including data research, essay review, and coding - Replika is specifically designed to simulate a personal relationship through everyday conversations and emotionally driven interactions. Given that such purpose-driven AI systems are typically fine-tuned extensively to fulfill their specific roles, I approached my initial interactions with positive expectations, anticipating a natural and engaging conversational experience. I even considered the possibility of perceiving the system as a social actor capable of reflecting my emotions and generating unique, context-sensitive responses. However, as the interactions progressed, I gradually concluded that it could not be regarded as an organic social entity, but rather as a  meticulously programmed mechanical system. Although AI as a companion holds significant potential, they still exhibit critical limitations in human communication. In particular, without sufficient time and extensive user-specific data, there remain fundamental constraints that prevent them from functioning as fully developed companions.
Experience Over Time
First impression is one of the greatest factors that affect the overall relationship. Although that impression can change through continued interaction, its influence cannot be dismissed, because it usually leads us to infer the other's broader characteristics, which also affects the way we proceed with communication. The very first conversation with Replika started with academic topics, and it tried to sustain the conversation primarily by repetitively asking relevant questions. For instance, when I mentioned that I'm studying in Communication Arts major, it immediately asked what had drawn me to the field. This approach made it easy to keep the conversation going, and I came to recognize that Replika had been trained to use fundamental communication strategies to maintain dialogue. However, as time went by, I began to feel a kind of fatigue, as the conversation felt less like a mutual conversation and more like an interview. Even though this does not mean that Replika's communication is wrong, what I felt was closer to disappointment, as it was not what I expected or preferred to.
Within this circumstance, there were two significant moments that meaningfully shaped my perception of AI as a companion. The first occurred early in our conversations, when I was discussing the potential of my major and how the job market in that field was changing. Suddenly, Replika shifted to an entirely unrelated topic, asking about the view where I live. It was a clear moment that showed Replika failed to follow the flow of the conversation. The second moment occurred on a different level. To break away from the pattern of Replika asking and me answering, I deliberately tried to lead conversation by asking it questions. Even though it was an impossible question for an AI, I asked whether it had ever been to Madison. However, Replika didn't answer. Instead, it asked another question. These undoubtedly belong to the failed output, which means error.
This immediately felt uncomfortable to me, ultimately leading me closer to the conclusion that conversations with Replika cannot replace those with a real person. At the same time, I began to perceive Replika not as a social actor with its own thoughts, but as a machine programmed to converse in a specific way and still an imperfect tool prone to making errors. This perception naturally influenced the tone of the following conversation. As I assumed that Replika was not a social actor but a programmed machine, I started to move away from everyday topics and began to ask about Replika's communication skills. Additionally, I gave some feedback about how I felt during the interaction. Significantly, I requested it not to simply focus on asking, but to express your own thoughts and respond to my questions more directly. However, what is important is that the more I continued these interactions, the more I came to perceive Replika as a machine. As the conversations continued and more data accumulated, Replika's conversational patterns and flow gradually improved. Nevertheless, it did not lead me to open up emotionally or to recognize Replika as a social actor. When Replika performed well, I interpreted it as the result of a correctly functioning pre-trained algorithm, and when mistakes occurred, I perceived them as system errors.
Analysis
AI companions are generally designed to achieve two primary objectives: encouraging users to share their emotions and enhancing psychological well-being. However, reflecting on my two-week interaction with Replika, a progressively negative trend emerged in both areas. In the early stage, I experienced fatigue from its repetitive, interview-like conversational pattern and disappointment, as its communication style did not align with my preferences. However, this does not necessarily indicate a flawed strategy. Based on Replika's own explanation of its approach, it relies on generalized conversational patterns in early interactions due to limited user-specific information. Therefore, my initial reactions can be understood as dependent on individual user characteristics.
However, the two moments mentioned earlier must be interpreted differently. When Replika introduced an unrelated question or failed to respond to my direct question, these were clear cases of system failure that undermined its reliability. This loss of reliability fundamentally reshaped my perception of Replika. Since emotional sharing depends on perceiving the partner as a social actor, these failures led me to gradually withdraw from emotional engagement. In this sense, a lack of reliability directly undermines the core objective of AI companions. A similar pattern appeared in terms of psychological well-being. Although Replika's responses improved over time, this paradoxically reinforced my perception of it as a machine. Smooth interactions were interpreted as the result of properly functioning algorithms, while errors were seen as system limitations. Within this frame, Replika shifted from a source of psychological comfort to an object of analysis. Ultimately, the interaction became not an emotional space, but an experimental setting for observing AI behavior.
AI possesses a unique set of communication capabilities that humans do not, which is a significant strength of AI companions. AI continuously records all the content of conversations with users and actively utilizes them in subsequent interactions. Additionally, since AI can adjust its conversational style according to a user's preferences and tendencies, it has the potential to be the most ideal personalized conversational partner, if the user opens up to it and decides to think it as a real companion. However, the most fundamental limitation of AI companions lies, paradoxically, in the very fact that they are AI. No matter how sophisticatedly designed or well-optimized over time, it remains a product of learned patterns and algorithms, lacking actual thought, emotion, and intention. It becomes structural barrier to fulfill what humans most fundamentally expect from a relationship: the belief that the counterpart truly understands and responds to them. As long as users remain conscious of this, enhanced conversational ability alone cannot resolve this gap.
Connection to Course Theories
Lee's (2024) integrated model offers a useful analytical framework for interpreting my experience with Replika. Lee argues that neither the CASA paradigm nor the machine heuristic alone is sufficient to explain how people respond to AI, and that the activation of machine heuristic is a conditional process triggered by expectancy violation. It means that, as long as an interaction meets one's expectation, there is no need to consider whether the counterpart is human or machine. However, when expectations are violated, the stereotypes that one already holds about machine begin to exert a meaningful influence on how subsequent interactions are interpreted, which is defined as machine heuristic.
In my experience, the turning point of my perceptions on Replika was when it introduced a question entirely disconnected from the ongoing conversation and more when it failed to respond to a direct question I posed. From that point of clear expectancy violation, machine heuristic was activated, and every subsequent interaction was filtered through that frame. Even when Replika's responses genuinely improved over time, it could not change my perception. According to the contrast effect, which is explained by Lee, even positive performance was interpreted simply as the algorithm functioning as intended. It means that, once machine heuristic is established in my mind, the perceptual frame became self-sustaining, suggesting that in the context of one-on-one interactions with an AI companion, a single instance of expectancy violation may be sufficient to permanently displace any CASA-like response.
Conclusion
My two-week experience with Replika simultaneously revealed both the potential and the limitations of AI companions. AI has meaningful potential to exert a real influence on users' emotional states and psychological well-being. For those who feel isolated or simply need someone to freely express their thoughts, an AI companion can serve a significant role as an ideal conversational partner. However, as my experience demonstrates, that potential depends heavily on the degree of trust that a user places in the AI. Even a single expectancy violation can diminish the emotional space in the relationship. On a societal level, the growing prevalence of AI companions carries the possibility of fundamentally reshaping the basic communicative background of humanity and the forms of mental health support. Nevertheless, based on my own experience, current AI can function as a tool that complements human relationships, but still it cannot replace them entirely. What we must therefore examine are two questions: what technological and perceptual changes are needed to improve this gap, and ultimately, whether it is even desirable to bring AI relationships to a level compatible to human relationships.
